What this does

  • Screens against the official restricted-party lists you enable — nothing is checked unless you turn it on.
  • Looks up the legal entity named in your request.
  • Compares national trade statistics relevant to the check.
  • Shows where each finding came from, and states plainly what could not be checked.

What this doesn't do

  • Give a sanctions clearance or legal advice. A due-diligence report is decision support for a person to review, not a legal clearance.
  • Check sources you haven't enabled, or anything beyond the official lists and the entity/trade data it names.
  • Replace your own compliance team's sign-off.
